Webber (1984): applied vs scientific modelling

Applied:

Day to day tools; longer-term forecasting, planning

“Regards the form of society as given”

Scientific:

“increase understanding”

“Integrate theoretical and empirical research”

“Examine the conditions under which the society operates and the manner in which it is changing ... such research ought not to regard the form of society as constant.”

An imaginary Social Technology…

The goal:

adapt a crop to suit growing in a particular landscape

Organising principle:

Decentralised network of growers

Addressing what problem?

Modern varieties bred for non-organic production

Land must be changed to suit crop - monoculture

Andean potato farmers did it already

“market-oriented experiment stations with good yields, great adaptability, and reliability” James Scott

Key features of this method:

Many growers - exchange information and cultivars

Start with plot conditions

Grow many varieties at once

Experiment in ‘chacritas’ – small gardens

Five year gap between new crosses and larger-scale growing

Taxonomy for communication

Balinese rice-growing

Irrigation coordinated over whole landscape

Through water temple system

Balances growth with pest control

Maximises yield
